Business Objective

The business objective of the project is to help students make better daily financial decisions by reducing the effort required to monitor and understand their finances. The application aims to give students a clearer understanding of their overall financial situation by making important financial information easier to access, organize, and understand. By improving financial awareness, the project aims to help students recognize where their money is going, identify potential spending problems earlier, and adjust their spending habits when necessary. Ultimately, the project seeks to provide students with a centralized and accessible way to better understand their finances and develop more responsible financial habits.

Strategy-to-Project Chain

The project supports this business objective by addressing problems identified during the team's market research. Student interviews identified recurring difficulties with manually tracking purchases, understanding where money was being spent, determining how much money was safely available to spend, and recognizing overspending before it became a larger problem. Some users described relying primarily on their bank balance rather than maintaining a structured budget, while others wanted spending to be automatically organized into categories and presented in a way that required little manual effort. These findings connect directly to the proposed financial management application. Rather than requiring students to manually collect and interpret financial information from multiple sources, the project will focus on making that information easier to access and understand. Features such as transaction tracking, spending categorization, budget monitoring, and visibility into available money support the broader objective of improving students' awareness of their financial situation.

Strategic Justification

. Interview responses showed that users experience difficulties monitoring spending and planning around limited or changing financial resources. For example, participants described small purchases accumulating unexpectedly, uncertainty about what they could safely spend, and a desire for earlier warnings before exceeding a budget. The proposed project responds directly to those findings by concentrating on visibility, organization, and reduced manual effort.
