Market Research

During Phase 1, our team researched different problems that could potentially be used as the main project for the semester. Instead of starting with one specific application, each team member explored different areas and interviewed people about problems they had experienced.

Download the Market Research PDF

Aggregate Findings

The main areas researched were college parking, calorie tracking, university social media, and personal finance management. Each area showed problems that could potentially be addressed through software.

College parking interviews showed problems with finding available spaces and the additional time required to look for parking. Calorie tracking showed that users can forget to enter information or stop tracking because the process becomes repetitive. University social media research showed difficulty finding current information about events, organizations, and students with similar interests.

Personal finance management showed a repeated problem with keeping track of spending. Several interviewees explained that smaller purchases can add up without being noticed until transactions are reviewed later. People already use tools such as Excel, notes, bank applications, and financial tracking applications, but maintaining this information can require time and consistency.

Top Pick, Deep Dive

After reviewing the different ideas and continuing the research during Phase 2, our team decided to move forward with personal finance management.

The main problem identified was not necessarily that people do not understand budgeting. The problem was consistently tracking spending and understanding how everyday purchases affect the amount of money available at the end of the week or month.

The research also showed that simply displaying transactions would not be enough because users can already obtain this information from their bank accounts. CampusCents would instead focus on helping users organize and better understand their financial information.

Based on the interviews, the project may include expense tracking, budget categories, savings goals, spending alerts, and transaction categorization. The goal is to reduce some of the manual work involved in managing personal finances while giving users a clearer view of their spending habits.

What Didn't Work / What We Pivoted From

College parking showed a real problem, but an accurate parking application could depend on access to university information or parking infrastructure. Calorie tracking also showed repeated problems, but many applications already focus on this area.

University social media showed problems related to finding events, organizations, and students with similar interests. However, the usefulness of this type of application would depend heavily on having enough students actively using the same platform.

The finance idea also changed during the research. The original idea focused mainly on tracking spending. After reviewing the interviews, we realized that users can already see their transactions through their banks, so tracking alone would not provide enough value.

Because of this, the project developed into a broader personal finance management concept focused on helping users understand their spending through categories, budgets, savings goals, and alerts.
