Interviews & Competitor Analysis

Competitor Overview

Competitor Competitor Type Primary Focus / Value Proposition
Cleo Direct Competitor AI-native conversational chatbot that uses conversational nudges, roasts, and automated rules to encourage saving.
Monarch Money Direct / Adjacent Competitor Full-suite household budgeting and financial tracking tool with integrated AI assistance.
YNAB (You Need A Budget) Indirect Competitor Strict manual zero-based budgeting system focusing on behavior change rather than automated AI.

Interview Script and Guidelines

Timeline Overview:

  • [00:00 - 02:00] Warm-Up & Context
  • [02:00 - 07:00] Product Usage & Core Mechanics
  • [07:00 - 12:00] AI/Automation Experience & Pain Points
  • [12:00 - 15:00] Decision & Value Assessment

Interview 1: Cleo User (Direct Competitor)

Goal: Understand how users interact with a conversational AI agent for spending and saving.

Questions:

  1. Background & Setup: How long have you been using Cleo, and what made you choose a chat-based app over traditional spreadsheets or bank apps?
  2. AI Engagement: How often do you interact with the chat interface (e.g., asking "Can I afford this?" or receiving auto-roasts)? Does the personality keep you engaged or ever feel gimmicky?
  3. Saving Nudges: Have Cleo’s auto-save rules or chat notifications actively stopped you from overspending? Can you give a specific example?
  4. Frustrations & Gaps: Where does the AI fail? (e.g., miscategorizing transactions, unhelpful responses, paywall friction)?

Interview 2: Monarch Money User (Direct / Adjacent Competitor)

Goal: Understand how users evaluate comprehensive tracking apps with AI query features.

Questions:

  1. Background & Setup: What primary goal led you to pay for Monarch Money (e.g., shared partner finances, net worth tracking, monthly budget)?
  2. AI Assistance Utility: Monarch has an AI financial assistant. Do you actively ask it questions about your finances, or do you rely mainly on the visual dashboards?
  3. Actionability: Does the app actively help you save money every month, or does it primarily function as a reporting mirror after spending occurs?
  4. Frustrations & Gaps: What feature is missing that would make budgeting feel less manual or tedious?

Interview 3: YNAB User (Indirect Competitor)

Goal: Understand why users prefer disciplined manual budgeting over automated AI tools.

Questions:

  1. Background & Setup: Why did you choose YNAB’s strict zero-based budgeting method over automated AI apps?
  2. Behavioral Impact: YNAB requires manual/intentional category assignment. How does that friction affect your daily purchasing decisions?
  3. Automation Views: Would you trust an AI agent to automatically allocate your income or budget categories for you? Why or why not?
  4. Frustrations & Gaps: What is the hardest part about sticking with YNAB, and where do you usually fall off or get frustrated?

Detailed Evidence Log

Artifact ID: LOG-01

  • Competitor Evaluated: Cleo (Direct Competitor)
  • Participant: CS Undergraduate Student (Uses Cleo for micro-savings)
  • Interview Duration: 15 Minutes
  • Teardown Completed: Yes (30-min onboarding & chat flow evaluation)

1. Verbatim Evidence & Observations

"I loved the 'roast' feature at first because it was funny, but after a month, asking the chat bot 'can I afford this' felt like too many steps when standing in line at a coffee shop. I just want the app to tell me my safe-to-spend limit automatically." — Participant 1

2. Teardown Findings

  • Onboarding takes <3 minutes with bank link.
  • High engagement initially, but chat interface adds interaction friction for quick checks.
  • Auto-save rules (round-ups) work silently in the background and are the most retained feature.

3. Product Takeaways & Actionable Decisions

  • Insight: Chat-only interfaces introduce friction for instant decision-making.
  • Decision for Our App: Implement a "Safe-to-Spend" widget/dashboard card for instant visual feedback, while reserving the AI Chat agent for deeper weekly summaries and personalized goal setup.

Artifact ID: LOG-02

  • Competitor Evaluated: Monarch Money (Direct / Adjacent Competitor)
  • Participant: Working Professional / Graduate Student
  • Interview Duration: 15 Minutes
  • Teardown Completed: Yes (30-min budget allocation & analytics breakdown)

1. Verbatim Evidence & Observations

"Monarch gives me amazing charts, but it's completely backward-looking. By the time I see I overspent on dining out, the month is already over. The app doesn't proactively intervene or help me adjust my budget mid-month." — Participant 2

2. Teardown Findings

  • Comprehensive financial aggregator, but requires heavy manual setup for monthly budget caps.
  • AI assistant feature is hidden deep in settings, leading to low feature discovery.
  • Lacks dynamic mid-month recalculations when a user overspends in a specific category.

3. Product Takeaways & Actionable Decisions

  • Insight: Static dashboards show history, but users need real-time behavioral intervention.
  • Decision for Our App: Build an AI Mid-Month Auto-Balancer that detects overspending early and suggests dynamic reallocations (e.g., "You went $30 over on dining out; move $30 from entertainment to stay on track?").
